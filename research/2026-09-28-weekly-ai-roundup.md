### 1. ChatGPT Voice: plugins (connected apps) and voice-started "Work" tasks
- **What happened:** On 23 September 2026 OpenAI added plugin support to ChatGPT's "Live" voice experience on web, iOS and Android. It also brought Voice into **ChatGPT Work** (on web and mobile), OpenAI's agent mode (an agent is AI that can carry out multi-step tasks for you, not just answer questions). In Work, you can ask out loud for documents, presentations or spreadsheets, use connected apps or work in a browser. If a task is still running when you end the call, it reportedly continues in text.
- **Plain-English explanation:** "Plugins" (also called connected apps) are links you set up between ChatGPT and other services, such as your email, calendar or Slack. Previously you mostly had to type to use them. Now you can just talk.
- **Why it matters:** Hands-free use is a real everyday benefit, for example while cooking or driving (with appropriate safety), or for people who find typing difficult. It also shows ChatGPT moving from "chatbot" to "assistant that does things".
- **Who could benefit:** Busy parents, small-business owners on the move, and people with accessibility needs.
- **Practical example:** "What's in my calendar tomorrow, and draft a reply to Sam saying I can do 3pm." Or, in Work: "Turn my notes from this call into a one-page summary document."
- **UK availability:** No UK restriction was mentioned in the sources found. Not confirmed on an official UK-specific page.
- **Pricing / free option:** According to search-engine summaries of OpenAI's "ChatGPT Voice" help article: Free and Go users can use Voice in Chat with the plugins their plan supports; Voice in Work needs both Voice and Work access; Voice in Work follows your plan's Voice limits and billing, and Work tasks count towards normal Work usage. The help centre describes Work as available on web and mobile "for eligible paid plans" and in the desktop app "when included for your plan and workspace". Exactly which plans include Work, and the limits per plan, are **not confirmed**.
- **Privacy and data:** According to the help-centre summary, "existing app permissions and workspace restrictions still apply", and you may need to connect and sign in to an app before it can be used. Speaking to an assistant that can read your email and calendar means it may read those out, or act on them, somewhere others can hear. Readers should check which apps are connected (Settings) and avoid connecting work accounts without their employer's permission. Secondary coverage mentions on-screen approvals before actions; this is **not confirmed** on an official page.
- **Verification status:** **Partially verified.** Re-check on 28 September: openai.com and help.openai.com returned HTTP 403 Forbidden, so the official pages still couldn't be opened. The date (23 September), plugin support in Live on web/iOS/Android, Voice in Work on web and mobile, and the Free/Go wording are consistent across search-engine summaries of the official release notes and "ChatGPT Voice" article, and with 9to5Mac and Unite.AI. The "continues in text" detail and plan-by-plan details are not confirmed officially.

### 2. GPT-6 Sol and GPT-6 Luna in ChatGPT
- **What happened:** On 22 September 2026 OpenAI released two new models (a model is the underlying AI "engine" that produces the answers). GPT-6 Sol is for demanding everyday work. GPT-6 Luna is for quick, high-volume tasks. According to OpenAI (search summary), they were trained with similar methods to GPT-6 Astra (released 3 September), bringing Astra's advances to faster, more affordable models.
- **Plain-English explanation:** These are faster, cheaper engines built from the same approach as OpenAI's top model. For most people, this means better answers inside ChatGPT's Work mode without paying for the top tier.
- **Why it matters:** OpenAI **claims** GPT-6 Sol makes "about half as many mistakes" as its predecessor. According to the search summary of OpenAI's page, this comes from OpenAI's own internal factuality evaluation, based on de-identified real conversations where users flagged mistakes. That is the company's own test, not independently established. Fewer errors would matter most for everyday tasks like summarising documents or drafting.
- **Who could benefit:** Plus, Pro, Business, Enterprise and Edu subscribers using ChatGPT Work, and Free/Go users willing to install the desktop app (Luna only).
- **Practical example:** A sole trader using ChatGPT Work to turn a messy price list into a tidy quote template.
- **UK availability:** Not stated as restricted. Not confirmed on a UK-specific page.
- **Pricing / free option:** According to OpenAI's announcement (search summary): available "in ChatGPT Work and Codex" for Plus, Pro, Business, Enterprise and Edu, rolling out gradually through the day. Free and Go users can use GPT-6 Luna in the **desktop app**. The models are "not yet available in Chat" (standard ChatGPT chat). API (developer) prices were cut by 50% compared with GPT-5.6 promotional pricing. That isn't relevant to most readers.
- **Privacy and data:** No change specific to these models was found. The usual ChatGPT data controls apply (for example, the setting that controls whether chats are used for training).
- **Verification status:** **Partially verified.** Re-check on 28 September: openai.com returned HTTP 403 Forbidden and community.openai.com was blocked, so the official announcement still couldn't be opened. Date confirmed by TechCrunch's URL date and multiple reports; availability wording and the mistakes claim come from search-engine summaries of openai.com.

### 4. Microsoft's rebuilt Copilot: Home, Code and Autopilot
- **What happened:** On 25 September 2026 Microsoft (Jared Spataro, on the Official Microsoft Blog) announced a redesigned Copilot built around:
  - **Home**, described as the new starting point "where Chat and Cowork come together" (Cowork is Microsoft's mode where you define a task and it runs end to end). With "Office in Copilot", Word, Excel and PowerPoint are built in, so from Home you can ask Copilot to draft a brief, model a budget or build a deck.
  - **Code**, which lets non-programmers build small, purpose-built tools (widgets, dashboards, internal apps) by describing them.
  - **Autopilot** (formerly "Scout"), a "persistent, proactive and personal agent". Microsoft says you give it "a name, a role and a goal", and it shows up where people already work: Teams, Outlook, chats, channels and documents.
- **Plain-English explanation:** Microsoft is turning Copilot from a chat box into a digital colleague that can make documents, build simple tools and chase things up for you.
- **Why it matters:** Many UK small businesses and schools already use Microsoft 365, so this is likely to reach them. Microsoft confirms two ways of paying: a per-user subscription licence (a fixed cost) covering Copilot in Chat and across Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Outlook and Teams, and **usage-based billing for Cowork, Code, Autopilot and frontier models**. That could make costs less predictable for small organisations.
- **Who could benefit:** Small businesses and organisations on Microsoft 365 business plans. Mostly business-focused, although Microsoft says Code will be "in preview for Microsoft 365 Premium and Pro subscribers later this year".
- **Practical example:** An office manager setting up an Autopilot agent to collect weekly updates from staff in Teams and draft a Friday summary.
- **UK availability:** Home and Code start rolling out to the **Frontier programme** (Microsoft's early-access scheme) "in the coming weeks". Autopilot "is expanding to private preview at the end of the month". **Not generally available yet.** The blog says nothing about regions or education availability, so UK timing isn't confirmed. (The previous version of this note cited Microsoft's EMEA newsroom; that page is still blocked and has been removed as a source.)
- **Pricing / free option:** Pricing model confirmed (above). No actual prices, and no UK prices, were given.
- **Privacy and data:** An agent that works in Teams, Outlook, chats and channels and acts on its own raises obvious data-governance questions for businesses and schools: who can see what, and who approves actions. Microsoft says code runs on "Copilot Managed Runtime", hosting "that lets code run safely right inside your company's Microsoft 365 environment" (in preview). That is Microsoft's description, not independently checked.
- **Verification status:** **Verified** (Official Microsoft Blog opened directly on 28 September): date, tab names, Scout→Autopilot rename, rollout wording and pricing model. UK/regional availability and actual prices are not stated.
- **Sources (checked 28 September 2026):**

- **Why it matters:** Mainly a structure change. Worth knowing so readers aren't confused by "Information Commission" in guidance. The ICO's 2026/27 plans (29 May 2026) include developing its AI code of practice, "dedicated guidance on agentic AI", and helping people navigate "an increasingly personalised AI landscape". Its technology guidance plan still lists the agentic AI guidance as "Drafting", with public consultation "due to launch September 2026" and final guidance due autumn 2026. **I could not confirm whether the consultation has launched.**
- **Who could benefit:** Schools, small businesses, anyone responsible for data protection.
- **My interpretation:** No action is needed for most organisations. It's a useful hook for a reminder about data protection when using AI tools.
- **Verification status:** **Verified** (ico.org.uk pages opened directly on 28 September). Open item: whether the agentic AI consultation has launched.
