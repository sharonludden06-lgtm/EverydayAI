Sunday evening. The week ahead has two after-school clubs, a late meeting, a birthday party and a dentist appointment, and somebody has just asked what's for tea on Tuesday.

This guide shows you how to hand the first draft of that problem to an AI assistant. You take two photos, send one message, and get back a meal plan built around what's already in your fridge and what your week actually looks like. It takes about ten minutes the first time and five after that.

It won't be perfect, and it doesn't need to be. The point is to get past the blank page so you can spend your energy on the decisions only you can make.

## What you'll need

- **An AI assistant that can look at photos.** ChatGPT, Google Gemini, Microsoft Copilot and Claude can all do this, including on their free plans, through their apps or websites. Free plans may limit how many photos you can add each day, but one or two is all this needs.
- **A photo of your fridge** (and your cupboard, if you like). Open the door, stand back a little and take one clear picture. Two or three is fine if things are hidden behind the milk.
- **Your week.** A photo of the kitchen calendar, a screenshot of your phone calendar, or simply a few lines typed out, such as "Monday: swimming until 6. Tuesday: I'm home late."

## Before you take the photos

A quick privacy check, because photos often show more than we mean them to.

- **Crop or cover anything personal.** Letters on the fridge door, a child's school name on a timetable, addresses, medical appointments with details, or other people's names.
- **Typing your week is often the safest option.** "Wednesday: after-school club, home at 5.30" tells the AI everything it needs without sharing who, where or which school.
- **Check the settings.** Most AI assistants let you choose whether your conversations are used to improve their services. It's worth a look in the settings or privacy section of whichever one you use.

## The message to send

Add your photos to a new chat, then copy this message, changing the parts in square brackets. (A message like this, telling the AI what you want, is often called a "prompt".)

> Here's a photo of my fridge and a summary of my week. Please plan evening meals for Monday to Friday for [2 adults and 2 children, ages 7 and 10].
>
> - Use what's already in the fridge first, especially anything that needs eating soon.
> - Our busiest nights are [Tuesday and Thursday], so make those meals 20 minutes or less.
> - [One child won't eat mushrooms. We don't eat pork.]
> - Keep it simple: normal family food, nothing that needs special equipment.
>
> Then give me a short shopping list of anything extra I'd need, grouped by supermarket aisle.

That's it. Send it and see what comes back.

## Making the plan fit your family

The first answer is a starting point. The useful part is the conversation afterwards, just as you'd chat it through with a friend who's good in the kitchen. Some replies you might send:

> Swap Wednesday for something the children can help make.

> Tuesday needs to be something I can prep on Sunday and reheat.

> Can you use the leftover chicken from Monday in Thursday's meal?

> We're trying to spend less this week. Can you make the shopping list cheaper?

Keep going until the plan feels like something you'd actually cook.

## Check what it tells you

AI assistants are good at this kind of planning, but they do make mistakes. They're confident even when they're wrong, so treat the plan as a helpful suggestion, not gospel.

- **It may misread the photo.** A jar of pesto can be mistaken for something else, and anything hidden at the back won't be seen at all. Glance down the list of ingredients it thinks you have and correct it: "There's no cheddar. That was butter."
- **It doesn't know your use-by dates** unless you tell it, and it can't smell the milk.
- **Allergies need a human check.** If anyone in your family has a food allergy, don't rely on an AI to keep them safe. Always check recipes and ingredient labels yourself.
- **Cooking times are estimates.** "20 minutes" can mean 20 minutes for a confident cook with everything chopped.

## Make it a habit

If the plan works, the second week is quicker still.

- **Keep the chat going.** Come back to the same conversation next Sunday and say "Same again for this week. Here's the fridge." It will remember your preferences within that chat.
- **Save your message.** Keep your version of the prompt in your phone's notes so you can paste it straight in.
- **Ask for a favourites list.** After a few weeks, ask: "Which of the meals you've suggested did we keep? Make me a list of our ten regulars." That list alone can make busy weeks easier.

## The human bit

An AI can suggest a sensible week of meals in seconds. It can't know that Thursday is the night everyone's tired and a bit fragile, or that fish finger sandwiches are sometimes the right answer. You still decide. The AI just saves you the staring-into-the-fridge part.
